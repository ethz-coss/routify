Sensitivity analysis — experiment setup
=======================================

Graph:     438,457 edges, ~6,148 km. GET /status/graph/, 2026-07-28.
Generator: ComputeFullRouting_v4.py
Sample:    N = 2000 O/D pairs (coordinates.json)


Parameters
----------
Each parameter is swept over 6 values, holding the other three at
their defaults. The value used by the existing test data is marked *
and is retained as a sweep step, so the existing runs slot in as
data points rather than being discarded.

slope (threshold, percent grade — edges steeper than the slider are
penalised; uphill only, per SlopeOperation)

   step   slider   penalised edges   penalised length
   ----   ------   ---------------   ----------------
     1      30           0.98%             0.39%
     2      19           2.17%             1.06%
     3      10 *         6.31%             4.28%
     4       7          10.25%             7.83%
     5       3          21.77%            18.39%
     6       0          39.07%            44.13%

green_index / noise / air (impact multiplier, [0,100])

   step    1    2     3     4      5     6
   -----   --   --   ---   ----   ---   ---
   slider   0   10    25    50 *   75   100

Uphill slope distribution (basis for the slope sliders), Jenks
natural breaks k = 5, fit on a 30,000-edge sample of positive
slopes, capped at 30% (drops 2.5% of uphill edges — altitude noise,
not roads):

   class   range (%)        edges    edges %   length %
   -----   -------------   ------   --------   --------
   1        0.00 -  3.22   80,074     47.94%     60.92%
   2        3.22 -  6.96   45,971     27.52%     21.92%
   3        6.96 - 11.99   24,600     14.73%     11.32%
   4       11.99 - 19.49   11,579      6.93%      4.45%
   5       19.49 - 30.00    4,822      2.89%      1.39%


Rationale
---------
The slope slider is a threshold, not a weight: it cuts the edge
population into penalised and unpenalised at the given grade, and
because SlopeOperation compares the signed slope, only climbs are
affected — 39.1% of edges are uphill, and that is the population the
slider partitions. Uniformly spaced slider values would sample that
population very unevenly, since it is heavy-tailed: half of all
climbs are gentler than 3.2% and only 2.9% exceed 19.5%. A uniform
grid would spend most of its steps inside the near-flat bulk, where
moving the threshold changes almost nothing, and would cross the
steep tail in a single step. Setting the sliders at the upper edges
of the Jenks classes instead makes each step move approximately one
homogeneous class of climbs into the penalised set, so the change in
penalised population is comparable from step to step and the
resulting curve measures the router rather than the sampling grid.
The one deviation is step 3, where the class-3 edge of 12 is replaced
by 10 — the existing default — a shift well inside the class width
and worth making so that no already-generated run is wasted. The
three impact parameters need no such treatment: they are linear
multipliers on a [0,1]-normalised value, so the response is smooth in
the slider and a graded set spanning both endpoints suffices, with
the spacing tightened at the low end where the operation transitions
from inactive to dominant and loosened at the high end where an
additional +25 changes little.


Budget
------
24 requests per pair (6 slope + 6 x 3 impact) -> 48,000 total.
Measured on dev-server: 1.45 s median per request, 914 KB median
response. Sequential: ~19 h, ~48 GB raw.
Recommended: write .jsonl, gzip, and reduce each response in-loop to
length-weighted metrics (distance, mean_slope, mean_noise,
mean_green, mean_pm10) keyed by (pair_id, param, step) — ~10 MB
final. Keep raw responses for a ~20-pair audit sample only.
